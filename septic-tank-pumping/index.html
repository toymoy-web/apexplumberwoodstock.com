Emergency Septic Backups: When The Tank Is Full

Slow drains throughout the house, sewage odors in the yard, or waste backing up into a tub or toilet can mean a full or failing septic tank. Cut back on water use and call our 24/7 emergency line.

We respond at any hour to pump the tank and tell you what we found. If the problem looks bigger than a full tank, we explain what to ask your county about next.

A typical residential septic tank needs pumping every 3 to 5 years depending on household size and tank capacity. Skipping that maintenance is how a manageable, predictable expense turns into a failed drain field, a genuinely larger and more expensive repair.

Georgia regulates septic systems through county boards of health operating under Georgia Department of Public Health rules, covering tank capacity, drain field placement. Inspection requirements, a different regulatory framework than the plumbing code governing a standard city-sewer connection.

Warning signs a tank needs pumping sooner than scheduled include slow drains throughout the house (not just one fixture), gurgling sounds, a sewage odor near the tank or drain field, or standing water in the yard above the drain field. Any of these is worth addressing before it becomes a backup into the house.

We pump the tank and provide a basic visual assessment of tank and lid condition as part of the service. We'll flag anything that looks like an early sign of a bigger issue, a crack, a failing baffle, rather than just emptying the tank and moving on.

What goes down a septic-connected drain matters more than it does with city sewer. Grease, harsh chemical drain cleaners, and non-biodegradable items can disrupt the bacterial activity a septic system relies on to break down waste, in a way city sewer treatment doesn't have to worry about.

New construction anywhere in unincorporated Cherokee County requires a septic system permit and a site evaluation by the county health department before installation. Selling a septic-connected home in Georgia generally requires disclosure of the system's condition, sometimes with a recent inspection.

Never attempt to open or inspect a septic tank yourself. The gases produced inside a septic tank can be genuinely hazardous, and tank lids are heavy for a real reason. Leave that specifically to a professional with the right equipment.

We coordinate with the county health department's records when needed for a property transaction, since Cherokee and Fulton County both keep septic permit and inspection history that can be relevant during a home sale.

A property with a garbage disposal on a septic system should generally use it more sparingly than a city-sewer home would. The extra solid waste adds to what the tank has to process between pumpings.

A septic-connected home's drain approach differs from a city-sewer home's. That is why we adjust our drain cleaning methods once a septic system is confirmed. A failing drain field sometimes first shows up as a general plumbing complaint before the septic connection is identified as the real cause.

This service is most relevant in the more exurban parts of our coverage area, particularly around Hickory Flat and the rural edges of Cherokee County.

Hickory Flat and the rural edges around Canton are the areas in our service map where septic systems are most common, alongside scattered rural properties elsewhere in Cherokee County.

Most of Towne Lake and downtown Woodstock sit on city water and sewer, so septic service is mostly a rural question. If your address is in the stretches toward Hickory Flat or Sixes Road, or outside the city limits, check your closing documents for a septic permit before you call so we know what to expect.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often does a septic tank need pumping?

Every 3 to 5 years is standard for most residential systems, depending on household size and tank capacity. A larger household or a smaller tank may need more frequent service.

What are the warning signs my septic tank needs pumping?

Slow drains throughout the house, gurgling sounds, a sewage odor near the tank or drain field, or standing water above the drain field are all signs it's time.

Is septic tank service regulated in Georgia?

Yes, through county boards of health operating under Georgia Department of Public Health rules, covering tank capacity, drain field placement, and inspection requirements.
