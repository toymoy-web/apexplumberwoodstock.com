Water Heater Repair in Woodstock, GA

Not every water heater problem means the unit needs to be replaced. A pilot light that will not stay lit, a heating element that has failed, or a pressure relief valve doing its job by venting are all repairable issues on a unit that otherwise has years of life left. Part of an honest repair visit is telling you which situation you're actually in.

☎ Call (770) 214-4545

Electric tank water heaters most often fail at the upper or lower heating element or the thermostat controlling it, which shows up as lukewarm water, water that runs out faster than it used to, or a tank that trips its breaker repeatedly. Both are straightforward, inexpensive repairs compared to a full replacement.

Gas tank units more often develop pilot light or thermocouple problems, where the pilot will not stay lit or the burner will not ignite even though gas is reaching the unit. That is a repair, not a replacement, in the large majority of cases we see.

A pressure relief valve venting water is not a leak to panic over; it is the safety valve doing exactly what it is designed to do when tank pressure gets too high, usually from a temperature setting that is too high or a buildup of sediment restricting normal pressure release. We check the setting and flush the tank before assuming the valve itself has failed.

The real decision point is age. A unit under 6 to 8 years old with a single failed part is almost always worth repairing. A unit past 10 years with a failed part, especially if there is any rust at the base, is usually more honestly served by a full water heater replacement rather than a repair that buys a few more months.

Most tank manufacturers offer a 6 to 12 year warranty on the tank itself, but that warranty typically covers the part, not the labor to diagnose and install it, and it requires the original proof of purchase and, on some models, proof of annual maintenance. We check for an active warranty before quoting a repair, since it sometimes changes the real out-of-pocket cost significantly.

If a repair visit turns up a tank that is corroding from the inside rather than a fixable part, we will say so plainly and walk through replacement options and real costs before doing any work.

A water heater that keeps tripping its breaker alongside other electrical oddities in the house is occasionally a wiring issue rather than the unit itself, which is outside plumbing scope, and we will tell you when a repair call is actually pointing at that instead.

We repair both tank and tankless units throughout Woodstock and into Hickory Flat the same day for most calls.

What To Check Before We Arrive

Gas smell near the water heater

Do not attempt to relight a gas water heater's pilot if you smell gas anywhere near it. Leave the house, call the gas utility from outside, and only call us once the gas company confirms it is safe to go back in.

Water pooling at the base

Water at the base of the tank itself, not a nearby fitting, usually means the tank is corroding from the inside and cannot be patched. Shut off the water supply to the tank and the power or gas to the unit until a technician arrives.

Breaker keeps tripping

An electric water heater that trips its breaker repeatedly should stay off rather than being reset again and again. Repeated tripping is the breaker doing its job to prevent a bigger electrical problem.

Frequently Asked Questions

My water heater is leaking. Is that a repair or do I need a new one?

Water leaking from a fitting or connection is usually a repair. Water leaking from the tank body itself means the tank has corroded from the inside and needs full replacement, since a corroded tank cannot be patched.

Why does my water heater smell like rotten eggs?

That smell usually comes from bacteria reacting with the magnesium anode rod inside the tank, particularly in well water. It's a repair (replacing the anode rod and flushing the tank), not a sign the unit is failing.

How do I know if it's worth repairing versus replacing?

Age is the main factor. A unit under 8 years old with one failed part is almost always worth repairing. A unit over 10 years old, especially with any rust at the base, usually costs more in repeated repairs than a straightforward replacement would.
