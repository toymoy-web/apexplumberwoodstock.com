A 1990s Community Now Aging Into Its Second Water Heater

Towne Lake Hills and the surrounding Eagle Watch and Wyngate sections were built mostly between the early and mid 1990s. That makes this one of the oldest planned communities we serve, not one of the newest.

That matters because a home's original water heater has a normal service life of 8 to 12 years, meaning most of these houses are already on their second or third unit. Any water heater still original to the house is now 25 to 30 years past its install date.

That age profile is why water heater replacement is the single most common call we get from this specific area, ahead of the citywide pattern (driven by the later 2000s building boom) that shows up elsewhere on this site. Copper and early-generation PEX supply lines are standard here rather than galvanized pipe. But original fittings and shutoff valves from a 1990s build are also reaching an age where they can seize or fail.

Emergency dispatch for a failed water heater or burst fitting is available around the clock, not limited to daytime hours.

What That Means For Drains And Leaks Too

An established neighborhood like this also means mature landscaping. Mature tree roots are the leading cause of sewer line problems in older sections of Cherokee County generally. A recurring backup in the same drain every few months is worth a camera inspection rather than repeated cabling.

The same clay-heavy soil that runs under the rest of this region sits under Towne Lake too. Slab leaks remain a real possibility in any slab-on-grade construction here, old or new. A water bill that rises with no obvious cause is worth a leak detection call rather than assuming it will resolve on its own.

Who's Responsible For A Leak: The Meter Or The House

City of Woodstock utility service draws a real line at the water meter: the city is generally responsible for the main up to the meter. The homeowner is responsible for everything from the meter into the house. That distinction matters in Towne Lake specifically because several of the original 1990s water lines run a longer distance from meter to house than in more recently platted subdivisions, giving a homeowner-side leak more pipe to develop a problem in.

We check both sides during a leak detection visit and tell you plainly which side of that line the problem is on before quoting a repair. It affects whether the fix is ours to handle or a call to the utility.

Towne Lake's proximity to Lake Allatoona and the Victoria Harbour marina also means a real share of homes here have irrigation systems and outdoor water features tied into the main supply, both worth mentioning when you call since they change where a leak search starts.

Before We Arrive: If You Have A Burst Pipe Or Active Leak In Towne Lake

Shut off the main valve first

In most Towne Lake homes this is a lever or wheel valve where the line enters the house, often in the garage. Shutting it off stops the water damage from getting worse while help is on the way.

Kill power to any wet area

If water is near an outlet, appliance, or the water heater's electrical connection, cut power to that circuit at the breaker panel if you can reach it safely.

Clear the immediate area

Move people, pets, and anything valuable away from the active leak, especially near an original 1990s water heater that may be failing at the tank itself.

Call our 24/7 line

Same-day dispatch is standard for an active leak or burst pipe in Towne Lake, not something you need to request specially.
