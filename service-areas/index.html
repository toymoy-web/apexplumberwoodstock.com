What Makes Each Area Different

We don't treat these as interchangeable dots on a map. Each area has a genuinely different housing profile and utility setup. That shapes what a technician actually brings on the truck before they even arrive.

Towne Lake (served by City of Woodstock water/sewer): Developed mostly in the 1990s through early 2000s (Towne Lake Hills homes commonly show 1993-1995 build dates), making it one of the older planned communities in this part of the county, not the newest.

Most homes here are copper or early-generation PEX rather than the galvanized lines found downtown. The supply lines and original water heaters are now 25 to 30 years old in the oldest sections, which is well past a water heater's normal service life and into the range where original copper fittings start failing too.

Hickory Flat (served by Cherokee County Water and Sewerage Authority (CCWSA)): A genuine two-tier mix. It combines a rural, unincorporated core with farmhouses and older homes dating back decades around the historic GA-140 crossroads, alongside some of the fastest new-subdivision growth in the county over just the last few years.

The older farmhouse-era homes are more likely to have original well systems or aging galvanized line if they predate county water service reaching the area. Meanwhile, the new subdivisions are entirely PEX or copper. A technician cannot assume pipe material here the way they might in a more uniform neighborhood.

Holly Springs (served by City of Holly Springs water/sewer (with Cherokee County Water and Sewerage Authority in surrounding areas)): One of the fastest-growing cities in the county: population went from about 3,200 in 2000 to over 16,000 by 2020. The overwhelming majority of homes here are new-construction, built well after 2000.

Because Holly Springs' housing stock is so heavily post-2000, PEX and copper are close to universal here. Galvanized pipe and cast iron drain lines, common issues elsewhere on this site, are genuinely rare finds in this city.

Sixes (served by Cherokee County Water and Sewerage Authority (CCWSA)): Anchored by BridgeMill, a large, established golf-course community built directly over the site of the old Sixes gold mine, with most homes dating to right around 2000 to 2006. BridgeMill's homes are large, mostly PEX or copper. Now old enough, roughly 20 to 25 years, that original water heaters and, in the larger homes, multiple bathroom fixtures are reaching typical replacement and repair age at the same time.

Canton (served by City of Canton water and sewer inside city limits, with Cherokee County Water and Sewerage Authority and private wells and septic outside them): The Cherokee County seat, up from 7,709 residents in 2000 to 32,973 in 2020. An older downtown core around the 1929 courthouse and the Etowah River sits beside a ring of post-2000 subdivisions. A technician brings older-pipe and camera gear for one and water heater stock for the other.

Acworth (served by Cobb County and the City of Acworth): A Cobb County lake city of 22,440 on Lake Acworth and Lake Allatoona, with more than 300 historic buildings downtown and a 1990s and 2000s housing wave now reaching water heater replacement age. Some Acworth mailing addresses sit in southwest Cherokee County, so we confirm the county first.

Kennesaw (served by Cobb County and the City of Kennesaw): A Cobb County railroad-town-turned-city of about 33,000, with a historic Big Shanty core around Depot Park and the Southern Museum and subdivisions built through the 1990s to 2010s. Two ZIP codes, 30144 and 30152, divide the downtown and Barrett Parkway area from the Kennesaw Mountain side.

That's also why each area above has its own dedicated page rather than a single generic "we serve everywhere" statement. Click through to the one closest to you for real, specific detail on housing era, pipe material, and the service that area calls about most.

Real ZIPs We Cover

Every ZIP code referenced on this site is specific to the location page it appears on, not a shared list copied across every area. Woodstock itself spans ZIPs 30188 and 30189. Towne Lake sits in 30189. Hickory Flat straddles 30115 and 30188.

Holly Springs receives residential mail through 30115 and 30188, since its own ZIP, 30142, is a commercial and PO Box code. Sixes, anchored by BridgeMill, is 30114. Canton uses 30114 and 30115, Acworth uses 30101 and 30102, and Kennesaw uses 30144 and 30152.

If you are not sure which ZIP your home falls into, that is a quick detail we can confirm on the call.

One Dispatch Line, Every Area

Regardless of which of these areas a call comes from, it reaches the same 24/7 dispatch line and the same written-quote-before-work-begins standard. We don't run a different, lesser standard for the outlying communities than we do for calls originating from inside Woodstock's own city limits.

If a job requires equipment or expertise we don't carry on a standard truck, an unusual commercial system, for example, we'll say so directly on the call rather than sending a technician out on a guess. That's true whether the call comes from a downtown Woodstock address or one of the surrounding communities above.

Getting Here Quickly, Wherever You Are

Response time depends on where a technician currently is on their route, not a fixed radius from a single shop location. Dispatching from multiple points across Woodstock and the surrounding communities, rather than a single central office, is part of how we keep response times reasonable across an area this size.

Cherokee County's road network, I-575 running north-south and a handful of state routes connecting the outlying communities, means drive times between these areas are usually shorter than the map distance alone would suggest. A call from Sixes and a call from downtown Woodstock on the same afternoon are both realistically reachable the same day in most cases.
