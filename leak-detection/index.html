Leak Detection in Woodstock, GA

Leak detection is consistently the highest-demand plumbing call category in this entire region, and the reason is Woodstock-specific: the clay-heavy soil under this city shifts more than sand or loam with seasonal moisture, and that shift is a real, recurring contributor to slab leaks in slab-on-grade homes.

Water Bill Went Up And You Can't Find Where It's Coming From?

That is the most common way this call starts: a water bill that jumped for no visible reason, or a meter that keeps spinning even with every fixture in the house turned off. Neither one tells you where the water is going, which is exactly what electronic leak detection is built to answer without cutting into a slab or a wall first.

A slab leak often shows up as a warm spot on the floor, the sound of running water when everything is off, or a section of flooring that stays damp for no obvious reason. None of those symptoms tell you where the leak actually is, which is why guessing and cutting open drywall or concrete on a hunch is the expensive way to find it.

We use acoustic listening equipment and pressure testing to isolate a leak's location before any cutting happens. Acoustic detection listens for the specific sound water makes escaping a pressurized line, and pressure testing isolates which section of the plumbing system is losing pressure, so the two methods together usually narrow a slab leak down to a small, specific area rather than an entire room.

A leak between the water meter and the house is a different situation from a leak inside the home's own plumbing, and the two are billed differently depending on where the responsibility line falls under Cherokee County's water utility rules. Part of the diagnostic visit is telling you honestly which side of that line the leak is on before any repair work starts.

Not every leak needs to be found this way. A visible drip under a sink or a running toilet does not need electronic detection, since the source is already obvious. Leak detection is specifically for the leaks you cannot see: slab leaks, leaks inside a wall, and underground leaks between the meter and the house.

Pressure testing works by isolating sections of the plumbing system with valves and monitoring how quickly each section loses pressure when sealed off. A section that holds pressure normally is ruled out; a section that drops faster than expected points to where the leak lives, which narrows the search before any acoustic equipment or cutting gets involved.

An underground leak between the water meter and the house is a different scenario from a slab leak inside the home's footprint, since it can show up as a soft or unusually green patch of yard, or water pooling near the meter box itself, rather than anything visible indoors. The same acoustic and pressure-based methods apply, just outside rather than under the slab.

Once a slab leak is confirmed, the repair itself is usually a spot fix rather than a full-home repipe, though a home with several unrelated leaks in a short window is sometimes a sign the whole system needs a broader look, which is a conversation worth having with a technician on site rather than guessing over the phone.

A leak that turns out to be at a fixture connection rather than inside a wall is often resolved as part of a routine drain or fixture visit instead of a full detection appointment, which is one reason we diagnose before quoting a price.

Slab leak calls cluster heavily in the older, downtown-adjacent parts of the city as well as newer builds near Sixes that sit on the same clay soil base.

Frequently Asked Questions

How can you find a leak without cutting into my floor?

Acoustic listening equipment picks up the specific sound of water escaping a pressurized line through a slab or wall, and pressure testing narrows down which section of plumbing is losing pressure. Together they isolate a leak's location to a small area before any cutting happens, rather than opening up an entire room to search.

My water bill went up but I don't see any water anywhere. Could it still be a leak?

Yes. A slab leak can run for weeks without any visible sign inside the house, since the water is escaping under the concrete rather than into a living space. A water bill that rises with no change in usage and a meter that keeps moving with everything off are the two most reliable early signs.

Is a slab leak covered by homeowners insurance?

Most policies cover the sudden, accidental damage a slab leak causes but not the plumbing repair itself, and coverage for a leak that went undetected for a long time can be denied as gradual damage. Check with your specific carrier before assuming either way.
