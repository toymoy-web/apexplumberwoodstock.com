Toilet Repair & Installation in Woodstock, GA

A running toilet, a weak flush, or a toilet rocking slightly on the floor are all common calls, and most have a specific, fixable cause rather than meaning the whole toilet needs replacing.

☎ Call (770) 214-4545

A toilet that runs continuously is almost always a worn flapper or an improperly adjusted fill valve, both inexpensive parts. A toilet that rocks slightly on the floor usually means the wax ring seal underneath has failed or the flange has cracked, which needs to be addressed before it leads to water damage on the subfloor below.

Older toilets in Woodstock's downtown homes sometimes use a different rough-in size or bowl shape than what's sold in stores today, which can turn what sounds like a simple swap into a slightly more involved fitting job. We measure before quoting a replacement rather than assuming a standard fit.

A toilet that clogs repeatedly, more than an occasional one-off, can point to a partial blockage further down the line rather than a problem with the toilet itself. If cabling the toilet's own trap doesn't resolve a recurring clog, that's worth investigating as a drain issue rather than replacing the toilet again.

New installations are usually straightforward, but older homes sometimes reveal a flange that's been raised or lowered by prior flooring work, uneven from the intended height. We check and correct that during installation rather than setting a new toilet on an already-flawed base.

Low-flow toilets installed to meet modern water-efficiency standards sometimes get blamed for weak flushing when the real cause is a partially clogged jet or an incorrectly adjusted flapper allowing too little water into the bowl. Both are quick fixes that restore a low-flow toilet's intended performance rather than requiring a swap to an older, higher-volume model.

A toilet installation is one of the more common DIY projects homeowners attempt themselves, and the most frequent mistake we see afterward is an improperly seated wax ring, which doesn't show up as a problem immediately but leads to a slow leak and subfloor damage over months.

A toilet that overflows is worth knowing how to handle before it happens: shutting off the small valve behind the toilet at the wall stops the water immediately, faster than trying to work the tank's internal flush mechanism while water is actively rising.

A toilet's supply line, the small flexible hose connecting the shutoff valve to the tank, is a common point of slow, easily missed leaks as it ages, worth a quick visual check during any other toilet service.

A toilet clog that keeps returning despite cabling the fixture itself sometimes points to a broader drain issue rather than the toilet. If a wax ring failure has already caused some subfloor water exposure, leak detection can confirm whether it has spread further than visible.

A whole-bathroom renovation involving a toilet swap is often a good time to also address any aging faucet or sink fixtures in the same room while walls and floors are already being worked on.

Older downtown Woodstock homes are the ones most likely to have a non-standard rough-in size worth measuring before ordering a replacement.

What To Check Before We Arrive

Know your toilet's shutoff valve

The small valve on the wall or floor behind the toilet controls water to that fixture specifically. Turning it clockwise stops an overflow immediately, faster than trying to work the tank's flush mechanism.

Stop flushing once you notice rising water

If water is rising in the bowl rather than draining, do not flush again hoping it clears. A second flush adds more water to an already backed-up fixture.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does my toilet run constantly?

Almost always a worn flapper not sealing properly or a fill valve set too high, letting water continuously trickle into the overflow tube. Both are inexpensive, quick repairs.

My toilet rocks slightly when I sit down. Is that serious?

It can be. A rocking toilet usually means the wax ring seal has failed or the flange is cracked, which can let water leak onto the subfloor over time if not addressed.

Can you install a toilet I bought myself?

Yes, we regularly install customer-supplied units. We'll check that the rough-in size matches your bathroom's existing plumbing before starting.
