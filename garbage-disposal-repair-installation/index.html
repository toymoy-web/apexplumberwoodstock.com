Emergency Disposal Repair: Leaking Or Jammed

A disposal that is leaking under the sink, backing water into the other side, or has jammed and stopped draining can take a kitchen out of use. Turn the disposal off at the switch, do not put a hand inside, and call our 24/7 emergency line if water is leaking.

We respond at any hour to clear jams, fix leaks, and replace a failed unit when repair is not worth it.

A humming sound with no spinning blade means the motor is receiving power but something is physically jamming the flywheel, most often a utensil, bone fragment, or fibrous food scrap. Many disposals have a manual reset button and a hex-key opening on the bottom specifically for clearing this kind of jam without professional help. That said, a jam that won't clear safely is worth a call rather than forcing it.

A disposal leaking from the bottom is typically a worn internal seal that's let water past the housing over years of use, a repair or replacement decision that depends on the unit's age. A leak from where the disposal connects to the sink flange above, rather than the unit body itself, is often a simpler mounting seal fix.

Disposals have a genuine service life, typically 8 to 12 years. A unit that's failed outright after that age is usually more cost-effective to replace than repair, especially since replacement units have improved noise and grinding performance over older models.

Installation for a new disposal is usually straightforward when replacing an existing unit. But a first-time installation under a sink that's never had one requires confirming there's an available electrical connection and, in some cases, a dedicated switch, which we check before quoting the job.

What goes into a disposal matters for how long it lasts. Fibrous vegetables (celery, corn husks), starchy foods in large quantities (rice, pasta). Grease are the most common causes of both jams and premature wear, even though a disposal is designed to handle general food waste.

A disposal that trips a home's electrical breaker repeatedly, rather than just jamming, points to a motor drawing more current than it should, usually a sign the unit is failing rather than just clogged. Is worth addressing before it causes a broader electrical issue.

Never reach into a disposal with your hand to clear a jam, even with the power switched off. Use the manufacturer's hex-key wrench or a long tool designed for the purpose. If you are not comfortable doing that yourself, that is exactly the kind of call worth making rather than risking an injury.

A disposal leak that turns out to be coming from further down the drain line, rather than the unit itself, is a drain cleaning matter instead. If a kitchen remodel includes a new disposal, it is a reasonable time to also check the faucet for the same age-related wear.

A disposal on a home served by septic rather than city sewer needs a different maintenance conversation, worth raising if that applies to your property.

We handle both quick jam-clearing calls and full replacements throughout Woodstock and into Towne Lake, Holly Springs, and the rest of our service area.

Disposals in the larger kitchens of Eagle Watch, Towne Lake Hills, and Bradshaw Farm see heavy holiday use. Disposal Leaks And Dishwasher Drain Connections

A disposal that leaks can be leaking at the sink flange, at the body, or at the drain connections underneath. Each one has a different fix, which is why we find the actual source instead of tightening everything. A leak from the body of the unit usually means replacement.

The dishwasher drain connects to the disposal or the sink drain, and it is a common place for slow drains and odors. We check the dishwasher drain hose and the air gap or high loop when we service the disposal, so the kitchen drains properly as a whole.

Frequently Asked Questions

My disposal hums but won't spin. What's wrong?

The motor has power but the flywheel is jammed, usually by a utensil or food scrap. Many units have a reset button and a bottom hex-key opening for clearing this. That said, we're glad to handle it if it won't clear safely on your own.

How long should a garbage disposal last?

Typically 8 to 12 years with normal use. A unit failing well before that age, or one that's jammed repeatedly despite reasonable use, may have an underlying issue worth diagnosing rather than just replacing.

Can I install a garbage disposal where there wasn't one before?

Usually yes, but we check for an available electrical connection and appropriate switch access first, since that's the main variable in a first-time installation versus a straightforward swap.
