A Railroad Town That Grew Into A City Of 33,000

Kennesaw began as Big Shanty, a railroad settlement from the 1830s, and took its current name under an 1887 charter. The Southern Museum of Civil War and Locomotive History, home of the General locomotive from the Great Locomotive Chase of 1862. The restored 1908 train depot in Depot Park anchor the older downtown. Homes and storefronts around that historic core hold the oldest plumbing in the city. Aging drain lines and original shutoffs are the usual trouble there.

Population growth tells the rest of the story. Kennesaw went from about 21,700 residents in 2000 to 29,800 in 2010 and 33,000 in 2020. A big share of homes date from the 1990s through the 2010s. In those subdivisions the typical calls are original water heaters reaching the end of their life, toilet and faucet fixtures after 15 to 25 years. Slab leaks where clay soil has shifted under a foundation.

Two ZIP Codes, Two Parts Of Town

Kennesaw uses ZIP 30144 and ZIP 30152. Downtown, Swift-Cantrell Park, and the Barrett Parkway and Cobb Place area sit in 30144, while Kennesaw Mountain National Battlefield Park is in 30152, along with the neighborhoods that reach toward the mountain.

Swift-Cantrell Park, a 42-acre park on Old 41 Highway, and the battlefield park are the landmarks most customers use to describe where they live. Homes on sloped lots near the mountain are where water tends to collect at the low point behind a house after heavy rain. A basement or crawlspace on that side of the city is worth a sump pump check. For a slab home that shows a rising water bill, slab leak detection comes first.

Cobb County Utilities, Permits, And The Same Georgia Code

Kennesaw is entirely within Cobb County, so water, sewer, and permits run through Cobb County and the City of Kennesaw rather than Cherokee County. The plumbing code is the same statewide standard we work under in Woodstock, the 2018 International Plumbing Code with Georgia amendments. For a water heater replacement or a repipe, we handle the permit through the right office where one is required.

Retail and restaurants around Barrett Parkway and the Town Center area also produce commercial calls, from restroom failures to grease-line backups. Our commercial plumbing and hydro jetting services cover them.

Dispatch From Woodstock To Kennesaw

Kennesaw is roughly 11 miles south of Woodstock, reached by I-575 and Bells Ferry Road. Usually a 20 to 30 minute drive depending on traffic. The dispatch line is open around the clock. Same-day response is standard for an active leak, a sewage backup, or a failed water heater.

A failed tank in a 1990s Kennesaw subdivision follows the same path as one in Towne Lake. Our water heater replacement crews carry both tank and tankless units. Customers in neighboring Acworth are on the same dispatch loop.

Before We Arrive: If You Have A Burst Pipe Or Active Leak In Kennesaw

Stop the water at the main

In most Kennesaw homes the main valve sits near the meter or inside the garage. Turn it fully closed, then open one faucet on the lowest floor to drain what remains in the line.

Keep power away from the water

If water has reached outlets, appliances, or the water heater, switch off that circuit at the breaker panel only if you can reach it without stepping through water.

Document before you clean

Take photos and short video of the damage before you mop or move anything. Insurance carriers usually want the untouched scene.
