Backflow Testing: What It Is And Why Some Woodstock Homes Need It Yearly

By the Woodstock Apex Plumbing team · April 16, 2026

Backflow testing sounds like a technicality until you understand what it is actually preventing: contaminated water flowing backward into your home's clean water supply, a genuine health risk rather than a bureaucratic checkbox that exists just to generate a service fee.

Backflow happens when pressure in a water system drops or reverses, potentially pulling water from an irrigation system, pool, or other outdoor connection back into the clean supply line instead of flowing outward as intended. A backflow prevention device is a one-way valve designed specifically to stop that reversal.

Homes with irrigation systems are the most common reason a backflow device is required at all, since an irrigation line often has chemicals, fertilizer runoff, or standing water that must never be allowed to reverse into drinking water. That is why the newer, larger homes with more elaborate landscaping around Towne Lake, Holly Springs, and BridgeMill are the ones we test most often.

Annual testing is not just a recommendation in many cases, it is a requirement, sometimes tied to a home's water utility account or a subdivision's HOA rules, particularly for irrigation-connected properties. A failed or untested device can technically put a property out of compliance even if nothing has visibly gone wrong.

The test itself is quick, typically 15 to 30 minutes, and checks that the device's internal check valves are holding pressure correctly rather than allowing any reverse flow. A device that fails testing needs repair or replacement, not just a note for next year.

Backflow prevention assemblies are certified against American Water Works Association (AWWA) and ASSE standards, and testing has to be performed by a certified tester using calibrated equipment, not just a general plumbing visual check. That certification requirement is part of why backflow testing is typically billed as its own service rather than bundled silently into another visit.

New construction with an irrigation system in Cherokee County generally requires a backflow device be installed and tested as a condition of final inspection approval, which is why virtually every newer home in the larger subdivisions around Woodstock already has one, whether or not the homeowner is aware of it.

A pool is a separate but related consideration: pool fill lines are another common backflow risk point, since pool water can contain chemicals that must never reverse into the drinking water supply. If your home has both irrigation and a pool, it's worth confirming both connections are properly protected rather than assuming one device covers everything.

A device that has never been tested since installation isn't automatically failing, but age matters: internal check valve components can wear or accumulate mineral deposits over years of use, in the same moderately hard water that affects other plumbing throughout this region, gradually reducing how reliably the device seals against reverse flow.

Documentation from a passed test is worth keeping on file, both for your own records and because a buyer's inspector or a utility audit may ask for proof of recent testing. A simple dated certificate from the testing technician is the standard form this documentation takes, and it's worth storing alongside other home maintenance records rather than discarding it once the test is done, the same folder where you'd keep water heater and HVAC service records.

What To Do Before You Call

Know if your home has a backflow device

Look for a device near where your irrigation line splits off from the main water supply, often above ground near the meter or where the irrigation system enters the yard. If you're not sure, ask during your next plumbing visit.

Don't ignore a notice from your water utility

Some utilities send annual reminders when a backflow test is due or overdue. Treat these as a real compliance deadline, not routine junk mail, since noncompliance can affect water service in some jurisdictions.

Never bypass or remove a backflow device

A device that seems to be restricting flow should be tested and repaired, not removed. Removing it entirely eliminates a genuine health protection, not just an inconvenience.

Common Questions

How much does backflow testing cost?

It's a relatively inexpensive test compared to most plumbing services, typically a fraction of the cost of a standard service call, precisely because it's quick and doesn't usually involve repair work unless the device fails.

What happens if my backflow device fails testing?

It needs repair or replacement before it will pass, since a failed device means it's no longer reliably preventing reverse flow. We can typically handle the fix in the same visit as the failed test.

Do I need backflow testing if I don't have an irrigation system?

Usually not, unless your home has another specific cross-connection risk, like a pool fill line or certain commercial or agricultural water uses. A standard home with no irrigation, pool, or similar connection typically doesn't require one.

What This Means For You

If your home has an irrigation system, a pool, or any outdoor water connection and you cannot remember the last time backflow testing happened, that is worth checking before it becomes a compliance issue rather than a routine appointment. A quick call to schedule the test is a lot simpler than dealing with a compliance notice or a device that fails at the worst possible time.

Backflow devices are typically inspected during the same visit as other outdoor plumbing work; ask about it alongside any water heater service call if your home has irrigation. A failing device can sometimes present as unexplained pressure changes elsewhere in the house, worth ruling out with leak detection if the cause is not obvious. That kind of on-site judgment call is exactly why we assess in person before quoting a fix.

The larger, more heavily landscaped homes near Holly Springs' newer developments are where we perform this test most often. Reach out and we can confirm your exact service area on the call.
