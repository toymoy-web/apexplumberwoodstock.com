Backflow Testing & Prevention in Woodstock, GA

Written by the Woodstock Apex Plumbing team · Updated October 11, 2026.

Backflow testing sounds like a technicality until you understand what it's actually preventing. It's one of the more common compliance requirements homeowners in our newer, larger subdivisions run into without fully understanding why.

Got A Notice That Your Backflow Device Needs Testing?

If a utility notice or an HOA reminder mentioned "backflow testing" and you're not sure what that actually means, here's the short version. It's an annual check on a device that prevents contaminated water from flowing backward into your home's clean water supply. It is most commonly required for homes with an irrigation system.

Backflow happens when pressure in a water system drops or reverses, potentially pulling water from an irrigation system, pool, or other outdoor connection back into the clean supply line instead of flowing outward as intended. A backflow prevention device is a one-way valve designed specifically to stop that reversal.

Homes with irrigation systems are the most common reason a device is required at all. An irrigation line often has fertilizer runoff or standing water that must never reverse into drinking water. The newer, larger homes with more elaborate landscaping around Towne Lake, Holly Springs, and Canton are where we perform this test most often.

Annual testing is frequently a real requirement, not just a suggestion, sometimes tied to a home's water utility account or a subdivision's HOA rules. A failed or untested device can technically put a property out of compliance even if nothing has visibly gone wrong.

The test itself is quick, typically 15 to 30 minutes, checking that the device's internal check valves hold pressure correctly. A device that fails needs repair or replacement, not just a note to test again next year.

Backflow prevention assemblies are certified against American Water Works Association (AWWA) and ASSE standards. Testing has to be performed by a certified tester using calibrated equipment. That is part of why this is billed as its own service rather than bundled into a general visit.

New construction with an irrigation system in Cherokee County generally requires a backflow device be installed and tested as a condition of final inspection, which is why most newer homes already have one installed, whether or not the homeowner realizes it.

If you ever notice discolored or unusual-tasting water immediately after your irrigation system has run, that is worth mentioning specifically when you call. It can be an early sign of a backflow device that is not sealing properly rather than an unrelated water quality issue.

A property changing ownership is a reasonable trigger to schedule a backflow test even if one isn't currently due. A new owner benefits from starting with documented, current compliance rather than an unknown history.

Backflow devices are often checked during the same visit as other outdoor plumbing work, including a water heater service call if your home has irrigation. A device that has failed can sometimes present as an unexplained pressure change, worth ruling out with leak detection if the cause is not obvious.

Commercial properties with irrigation, fire suppression, or certain kitchen equipment have their own backflow compliance needs, covered under our commercial plumbing service.

What To Check Before We Arrive

Don't ignore a compliance notice

A notice that your backflow test is due or overdue is a real deadline, not routine mail. Ignoring it can affect water service in some jurisdictions.

Never remove or bypass a backflow device

A device that seems to restrict flow should be tested and repaired, not removed. Removing it eliminates a genuine health protection, not just an inconvenience.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need backflow testing if I don't have an irrigation system?

Usually not, unless your home has another specific cross-connection risk, like a pool fill line. A standard home with no irrigation, pool, or similar connection typically doesn't require one.

How often does backflow testing need to happen?

Annually, in most cases, sometimes tied to a water utility account or HOA requirement specific to your property.

What happens if my device fails the test?

It needs repair or replacement before it will pass. We can typically handle the fix in the same visit as the failed test.
