Emergency Faucet & Sink Repair in Woodstock, GA

Written by the Woodstock Apex Plumbing team · Updated October 11, 2026.

A dripping faucet wastes real money over time and is usually a worn cartridge or O-ring rather than a sign the whole fixture needs replacing. That said, a fixture original to an older home sometimes reaches the point where parts are no longer available.

Emergency Faucet And Sink Leaks: Stop The Water First

A burst supply line, a broken valve under the sink, or a faucet that will not shut off is an emergency. Close the shutoff valve under the fixture, or the main water valve if that will not turn, and call our 24/7 emergency line.

We handle emergency faucet and sink repairs at any hour, from replacing a failed shutoff valve to repairing a split supply line, and we leave you with working water.

Most modern faucets use a cartridge system that can be replaced without swapping the entire fixture. That's the fix we check first on a dripping or hard-to-turn faucet. Older two-handle faucets with separate hot and cold valves sometimes use compression washers instead, a different but equally repairable mechanism.

Low water pressure at a single faucet, rather than throughout the house, usually means a clogged aerator (the small screen at the tip of the faucet) rather than a supply line problem. That's a two-minute fix once identified, and worth ruling out before assuming a bigger issue.

A sink that drains slowly but isn't fully clogged often has a P-trap partially blocked with soap scum, hair, or food debris, especially in a kitchen sink without a garbage disposal. That's a straightforward cleanout rather than a full drain cleaning call in most cases.

Fixture replacement makes sense when a faucet is old enough that replacement cartridges are discontinued, when there's visible corrosion at the base, or when a homeowner simply wants to update a bathroom or kitchen's look during other work. We can usually install a customer-selected replacement without touching the surrounding countertop.

A kitchen faucet's pull-down or pull-out sprayer hose is a common point of failure separate from the faucet body itself, developing a kink or a worn connection that causes it to retract slowly or leak at the base. That's usually a hose replacement rather than a full faucet swap.

Bathroom sink stoppers (the pop-up mechanism) can seize or stop sealing properly over years of use, which shows up as a sink that won't hold water for shaving or soaking. That mechanism is separately repairable from the faucet itself and often gets overlooked during a general faucet repair call.

A faucet that suddenly sprays unevenly or in the wrong direction, rather than just dripping, often means the aerator has come loose or a cartridge has shifted slightly out of position, both quick fixes rather than a sign of bigger damage.

A bathroom sink's overflow drain, the small hole near the top of the basin, can also develop a slow leak at its internal connection over time, a less commonly checked source of a mysterious under-sink cabinet leak.

A pedestal or wall-mounted bathroom sink sometimes has less accessible plumbing underneath than a standard vanity sink. That can add modest labor time to an otherwise routine repair, something we account for in the quote rather than treating every sink the same regardless of its mounting style.

A sink drain that stays slow even after clearing the P-trap is worth a proper drain cleaning visit rather than repeated DIY attempts. If a faucet replacement is part of a broader bathroom update, it is often a good time to check a toilet for the same age-related wear.

Outdoor hose bib faucets follow similar repair logic to indoor fixtures but are worth mentioning specifically before a hard freeze. A frost-free spigot left with a hose attached defeats its own freeze protection.

This is one of the most common calls across every part of our service area, from downtown Woodstock to the newest homes in Holly Springs. Faucet wear happens on the same general timeline regardless of a home's age.

Homes in established neighborhoods such as Towne Lake Hills, Wyngate, and Brookshire have often been remodeled around original plumbing. Mismatched supply lines and old shutoffs behind cabinets are the usual surprise. The oldest homes near downtown Woodstock add the occasional galvanized stub-out under a sink, which we replace rather than patch.

Supply Valves, Shower Valves, And Outdoor Spigots

A burst or stuck supply valve under a sink or behind a toilet is one of the most urgent small repairs we see,. That is because the water does not stop until the main is shut. If a valve is leaking or will not turn, close the main water valve for the house and call. A new angle stop is a quick fix when we arrive, and it gives you a working shutoff for next time.

Shower valves and tub spouts that drip, run hot and cold at the wrong times, or leak into the wall behind the tile are a different job. We check whether a cartridge replacement will cure it or whether the valve body itself has failed. When tile has to be opened, we explain the access first so the repair does not turn into a surprise.

Outdoor spigots are another common call, especially after a cold spell. A split spigot can leak inside the wall without much showing outside, so we check the connection behind it as well as the faucet.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is my faucet dripping even after I tighten the handle?

A worn cartridge or O-ring inside the faucet body, not the handle itself, is the usual cause. Tightening the handle harder doesn't fix a worn internal seal and can eventually damage the handle mechanism.

Can you replace just the faucet without redoing my whole countertop?

In almost every case, yes. A standard faucet replacement uses the same mounting holes as the original and doesn't require any countertop work.

Why does only one faucet in my house have low pressure?

That's almost always a clogged aerator at that specific faucet rather than a house-wide pressure problem, since a whole-home issue would affect every fixture, not just one.
